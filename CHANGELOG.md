# Changelog

All notable changes to Esmbler are documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html). Each completed gate is tagged `gate-N-complete`.

## [Unreleased]

Gate 2: private pilot (in progress).

### Added

- Email intake (ADR 0023): mail to a workspace mailbox opens a record for its sender, with the sender's own permissions, and the requester's replies in the thread become their public replies (marked Email in the conversation), reopen a resolved ticket, or start a follow-up when it is closed; anyone else who answers by email, such as an agent replying to an escalation email, adds an internal note. Replies find their ticket by the email's headers or the ticket number in the subject; quoted history is dropped. Mailboxes receive through a webhook (`POST /api/mail/inbound` with the mailbox's token) or by IMAP polling from an allow-listed server, and each message is processed once however often it arrives. Automatic replies, bounces, mailing lists and the platform's own mail are never processed; senders the workspace's mail server did not verify, people who are not members and senders over 20 messages an hour wait on the Inbound email page (`/mail`), where agents read the reason and accept or reject; accepting acts as the sender only when the sender was verified, otherwise the agent acts with their own rights on the sender's behalf (naming a member for unknown senders), and accepted thread replies become the agent's internal note. Administrators manage mailboxes there. Inbound mail is kept 30 days. Attachments are listed but not yet imported. REQ-BIZ-003, REQ-EVT-007, REQ-ACC-13
- Notification email threads: every email carries its own Message-ID, refers to the message that opened the record and names the record's mailbox as Reply-To, so requesters can answer it; every notification is marked `Auto-Submitted` so auto-responders stay quiet. Templates can show the text of a public reply with `{{activity.body}}` (never internal notes, never to people outside the workspace), and people are not emailed their own replies. REQ-BIZ-002, REQ-BIZ-003
- Tickets 1.9.0: tickets by email (intake `core.tickets.mail.tickets`), the requester is told by email that a ticket was opened from their message, and agents' public replies reach the requester with the reply text. Upgrading changes no permissions. REQ-BIZ-003
- Alerts (ADR 0022): alert rules turn matching events into alerts with a lifecycle. Repeats of one problem count as occurrences on one alert (one per rule and key, by default per record); each alert has an owner (a group, a user or group field of the record, or a person), escalates on timers until someone acknowledges it (the last step may repeat), resolves when a resolving event happens (for example the ticket is resolved) or by hand, and reopens within a cool-down. Maintenance windows of up to 14 days record alerts without announcing them, and announce the ones still open when the window ends. Alerts never send messages themselves: they publish `platform.alert.opened`, `.acknowledged`, `.escalated`, `.assigned` and `.resolved`, and notification routes reach the alert's owners and assignee with the new recipient kind `alert_owner`. The Alerts page (`/alerts`) lists active, silenced and resolved alerts with acknowledge, assign to me, resolve with a note and a timeline, plus maintenance windows; the navigation shows a badge of open alerts that are yours. Overlay ops `alert_rule.add|update|remove`; MCP tools `list_alert_rules`, `add_alert_rule`, `update_alert_rule`, `list_alerts`; REST `/api/alerts`, `/api/admin/alerts/suppressions`; platform actions `alerts.respond` and `alerts.manage`; migration 0015. REQ-ALR-001
- Audit policy (ADR 0022): every workspace's audit entries are sealed into a SHA-256 hash chain that shows where an entry was changed or removed; the chain is verified daily and on demand. Details show "restricted" for values a viewer could not read in the app. Retention per workspace (365 to 3,650 days, seven years by default) removes only old, sealed entries and keeps the rest verifiable; legal holds pause every deletion in the workspace. The audit log page (`/admin/audit`) searches entries, exports CSV, or JSON Lines with each entry's chain link for offline verification (permission administrators only), shows the chain's state with "Verify now" and manages retention and holds. REST `/api/admin/audit`; platform actions `audit.export` and `audit.manage`; migrations 0016 and 0017; the worker runs sealing, verification and retention. REQ-AUD-001
- Retention for notifications and alerts: final notification deliveries and their attempts are removed after 90 days, inbox items 30 days after being read (unread ones after a year), and resolved alerts after a year, never during a legal hold; every removal is audited. REQ-NOT-005
- Tickets 1.8.0: an SLA target at risk or breached raises an alert for the ticket's assignment group, who hear about it in the app and by email; a breach nobody acknowledges within 30 minutes escalates urgently; the alerts resolve when the ticket is resolved, closed or cancelled, and a breach replaces the at-risk alert. Upgrading keeps workspace routes and changes no permissions. REQ-ALR-001, REQ-BIZ-005
- Notifications (ADR 0021): subscriptions say which events matter, routes say who hears about them, profiles say through which channels (in the app, email, text message) and templates say what they read, with shorter text per channel. Each person gets the message rendered under their own permissions; people outside the workspace see only public events and the fields an administrator allowed. Mandatory routes cannot be muted. Everyone has an inbox at `/notifications` with a bell badge, read state, links to records and "What I receive" to mute optional notifications or single channels. Administrators manage the environment's email and SMS channels (live, capture or disabled; SMTP and a Twilio-style SMS API; credentials as secret names), see every delivery with its status and attempts, replay failed or ambiguous ones, send a test message and preview a route without sending anything at `/admin/notifications`. MCP tools `list_notification_setup`, `preview_notification`, `list_notification_deliveries`, `notify_when`, `add_subscription`, `add_notification_template`, `update_notification_template`, `add_notification_profile`, `add_notification_route`, `update_notification_route`; REST `/api/me/notifications`, `/api/me/notification-preferences`, `/api/admin/notifications`; options `Esmbler__Notifications__AllowedEmailHosts`, `AllowedSmsHosts` and `MaxRecipientsPerEvent`; migration 0014. REQ-NOT-001, REQ-NOT-002, REQ-NOT-003, REQ-NOT-004, REQ-NOT-005, REQ-NOT-006, REQ-NOT-007, REQ-EVT-009
- Tickets 1.7.0: escalations go to the assignee and the assignment group in the app and by email, and a ticket becoming P1 is a mandatory urgent notification (app, email and text message where a number is known) that nobody can mute. Upgrading keeps workspace routes. REQ-NOT-004
- Create forms show an "Also needed" card for fields that are required right now (for example impact on a P1 ticket) but that the form's layout does not place, so the form can always be completed. REQ-WS-001

They name it, describe what they want (the matching starter is suggested), answer only the questions without a default, review the connected forms, explorers, dashboard, navigation, permissions and catalog, and create it. Tickets offers "IT ticketing": the latest release is installed, the team that receives new tickets is named from the answer, and the creator becomes administrator and joins that team. Modules declare starters in the SDK (`StarterSpec`). REST `GET /api/starters`, `POST /api/workspaces` (201 with the new workspace); options `Esmbler__AllowWorkspaceCreation` (off by default; Docker Compose turns it on for local development) and `Esmbler__MaxWorkspacesPerPerson`; migrations 0012 and 0013. REQ-WS-001, REQ-WS-002, REQ-ACC-15

### Changed

- SDK (additive): `SpecCatalog` gains `MailIntakes` (`MailIntakeSpec`), with `MailConventions.ReplyMarker` and `MailReasons`; `ChannelMessage` gains `MessageId`, `References`, `ReplyTo` and `AutoSubmitted` (true by default), which channel adapters may ignore. The architecture test keeping MailKit and MimeKit in `Esmbler.Kernel.Infrastructure` now also checks `Esmbler.Api`.
- SDK (additive): `SubscriptionTargets.All` gains `alert`, `RecipientKinds.All` gains `alert_owner`, `PlatformActions.All` gains `alerts.respond`, `alerts.manage`, `audit.export` and `audit.manage`, and `SpecCatalog` gains `AlertRules`; code that switches over these lists should expect the new values.
- SDK: `NotificationTemplateSpec.Event` and `Entity` are now nullable (`string?`), and templates gain `Label`, `Variants`, `ExternalFields` and `Enabled`. Stored releases and compiled modules work unchanged; C# module authors must handle null when reading `Event` or `Entity` (ASM-086). Templates with their own trigger keep working as before.
- Undo in the AI builder: any single step can be undone or redone alone again, and "Undo change" undoes or redoes everything one request did. MCP `undo_step` undoes the whole change unless `only_this_step` is set and reports only the steps it undid (ASM-065). REQ-LIVE-003
- Each bar, table row and explorer group header now stops counting at 10,000 with its own "+", like the list it opens.
- Tickets 1.6.0: the Unassigned saved view now uses the `unassigned` metric, so it also leaves out resolved tickets (1.5.0 left out only closed and cancelled ones) and lists exactly what the Overview tile counts (ASM-068). All open now uses the `open` metric with the same filter as before.
- Home now opens the first dashboard a person can see instead of the ticket list.

### Security

- Email intake (ADR 0023): a sender is verified only by the topmost `Authentication-Results` header of the mail server the mailbox names (comments and quoted strings ignored; DMARC for exactly the From domain, or aligned DKIM or SPF `smtp.mailfrom`); turning verification off needs permission administration; accepting an unverified message never acts as its claimed sender; only the requester's own email becomes a public reply; the per-sender cap counts processed mail only, so forged mail cannot use up a member's allowance; HTML conversion is capped at 512 KB with linear-time patterns; webhook tokens are stored as hashes and shown once; IMAP servers must be on an operator allow-list; mailboxes and inbound messages have FORCE RLS. REQ-BIZ-003, REQ-TEN-001
- Alerts and audit: the runtime role can no longer preset chain columns (column-level `INSERT`), and a trigger makes sealed audit entries immutable for every database role; audit export needs `audit.export`, is limited to 2 a minute per person, 400 days and 500,000 entries, is audited before the first byte and escapes spreadsheet formulas; AI agents never read, export or manage the audit log and never acknowledge, resolve, assign or silence alerts; alert titles and keys cannot read hidden fields or restricted event values, and alerts about a record are visible only to people who can read it; disabling or narrowing an alert rule behind a mandatory or external alert route needs permission administration to publish; retention keeps minimums in the database (equal to the defaults) whatever the caller asks, never deletes past the last verified link or a break and re-verifies what it deletes, and shortening it needs permission administration; audit details show "restricted" for values the viewer could not read in the app, JSON Lines exports (unredacted, with chain links) need permission administration, CSV escaping also covers formula characters after leading whitespace, `|` and `%`, Verify now is rate limited, and only permission administrators or the person who placed a legal hold can release it; alerts held by a maintenance window are announced when it ends. Threat model T-77 to T-80.
- Notifications: live email and SMS need an operator allow-listed provider host (none for email by default), checked again on every send; a sandbox channel goes live only for someone with permission administration; channel settings refuse anything that looks like a secret and store secret names only, whose values are never returned, audited or logged; sending to people outside the workspace, changing what they may see and making a route mandatory or optional are permission changes AI agents cannot make; one event reaches at most 200 recipient-channel pairs (audited when capped), each channel instance has a rate per minute, and deliveries are deduplicated per event, recipient and channel; a send with an unknown outcome is marked ambiguous instead of being repeated; message text in the delivery history is visible only to permission administrators (people who only manage notifications, auditors and agents see status); AI agents have no inbox and cannot manage channels or deliveries.
- Notifications review fixes: subscription conditions cannot read fields some people may not see (`config.notification_hidden_field`), and values of restricted events never appear in conditions or message text (`config.restricted_event_text`; an event without a sensitivity counts as restricted); email fields of a record count as external recipients; silencing a mandatory route or widening one with external recipients through its recipients, subscription, template, profile or the rules and transitions that publish the event needs permission administration to publish; modules route only through their own and the platform's notification elements; before each send the recipient is checked again (still an enabled member who can read the record, otherwise `no_longer_authorized`) and email goes to their current address; a mandatory route reaches a person even when another subscription's muted route got there first; deliveries suppressed because someone muted them or lost access are never replayed, and an ambiguous email is replayed only with force; recipients are capped per route before permission checks; the per-channel rate limit holds under parallel deliveries; previews are judged with the caller's real rights (agents need `records:read` to preview with a record), hide recipients from fields the caller cannot see and no longer reveal why someone would not be told; channel credentials are per workspace environment (`ESMBLER_SECRET__<WORKSPACE_SLUG>__<ENVIRONMENT_KEY>__<NAME>`, so a sandbox never reads production's), `*Ref` settings must name declared secrets, SMTP never sends unauthenticated when credentials are missing (unless the instance's `auth` is `none`), and the SMS client does not follow redirects; the channel form keeps settings it does not edit. REQ-NOT-001, REQ-NOT-002, REQ-NOT-004, REQ-NOT-005, REQ-NOT-006, REQ-AUTHZ-008
- Workspace creation: self-service creation is off unless an operator turns it on (`Esmbler__AllowWorkspaceCreation`); each person may create at most 5 workspaces by default, checked and inserted atomically by the database function `kernel.create_workspace` under a per-person lock, which also refuses anyone but an enabled person and fails closed on missing input; a new workspace stays hidden (`provisioning`) until provisioning finishes (`kernel.activate_workspace`), and a failed one stops counting toward the limit after 15 minutes; AI agents cannot create workspaces; the creator is taken from the session, never from the request; names and team answers lose control and bidi-override characters; creation has its own per-person rate limit (3 a minute) and is audited (`workspace.create`).
- Metrics count only records the viewer can read, inside the same SQL as the explorer; a figure that reads a field hidden from the viewer, or whose roles the viewer lacks, is removed rather than shown, and role gating never widens access. Drill links carry filters, never results, and the server re-applies the viewer's permissions; agents need `records:read` to evaluate a metric, and the result is wrapped as untrusted data.
- Dashboards, metrics and explorer group counts are rate limited per person (`Esmbler__MetricRequestsPerMinute`, 120 a minute by default); a list or group request takes at most 20 filters (`too_many_filters`); explorer totals and figures stop counting at 10,000 ("10,000+"); a dashboard request has an 8 s budget in which each statement gets at most 5 s, and figures not reached report a timeout; the empty-workspace check runs within that budget, and on timeout the real figures are shown. Changing who sees a metric, and undoing or redoing a permission change or any step of a change that contains one, needs permission administration (agents cannot); grouping options on fields hidden from the viewer are removed from the list they receive, and `describe_metric_fields` offers only fields the caller can see.
- Events: configuration cannot publish platform events (`record.*`, `platform.approval.*`, `config.published`) or another module's events, payloads cannot carry fields some people may not see unless the event type is restricted, and module-declared events on a transition cannot be removed by an overlay. Replays are audited with the consumer and event; retention keeps unreplayed dead letters and never shortens the replay window below seven days.
- Approvals: nobody decides on a request they made or that is for them, directly or through a delegate; one person's authority counts once per stage; AI agents cannot approve, reject or delegate; rejecting needs a reason; denied decisions are audited.
- People cannot step around approval: requests that need approval cannot leave intake except to be rejected or cancelled, cannot leave the approval state except to be cancelled, and migrations cannot skip or strand approvals. Changing which policy approves an item, or a used policy's approvers, needs permission administration.
- An escalated stage is decided by the escalation alone, so an escalation's rejection rejects the request.
- Approvers read only the requests they decide on, through a platform-maintained field no rule, transition or default can write; delegates read them only while the delegation is active.
- Request answers are frozen once a request leaves intake, and fulfillment task links can be written only by fulfillment, so conditional tasks such as a security review cannot be skipped or faked.
- Restricting or disabling a workflow transition applies to running records too; adding a transition anyone may fire, or changing roles, guards or set values, is a permission change (agents cannot make it).
- Migrate and cancel policies apply only to the publish that sets them and never on rollback; the migration job re-checks the publisher's rights.
- Workflow definition caching is per tenant and environment; platform writes are marked `source: system` in the audit trail.

### Fixed

- The create form no longer blocks on required fields it does not show, such as the requester: it checks and sends only its own fields, the server applies defaults, and errors on other fields appear as a message.
- The request form sends its configuration revision, so a catalog change while it is open asks the requester to review the updated questions instead of failing on stale ones.
- Module upgrades that skip a release now run that release's data migrations too.
- The sign-in page shows the demo account only where demo data is seeded (`GET /api/auth/sign-in-options`, KI-011).
- The production web build no longer depends on an inline `onload` script to load its stylesheet; the CSP blocked it, so deployed pages rendered without most styles. The CSP no longer allows Google Fonts hosts; Inter is served from the app's own origin.
